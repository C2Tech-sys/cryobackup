2 · How Orbitron is used

The reason Orbitron reads "game launcher" in most mockups is that it gets used for everything. Here it is rationed to five jobs, each with a fixed size and tracking, and it never appears below 9.5 px or in a sentence.

Sentences, table cells, forms, buttons, the email body and the tray text stay in the interface face. Orbitron's wide, geometric letters are built for a word or a number at a time; a paragraph of it is slow to read, and that is the exact moment a product tips from "console" to "launcher".
Hostnames stay in Plex Sans 600, not Orbitron, so FRONTDESK-01 and FR0NTDESK-O1 cannot be confused.
Timestamps and sizes stay in the mono face; Orbitron digits are for totals, not tables.
State chips stay in the interface face: they must read at 11 px.
Weight floor is 600; Orbitron 400 looks thin and dated on dark grounds.

Icon states

The tray icon is the mark, tinted by state. Users learn one glyph; the colour tells the story.

"Restore my files" opens a minimal local wizard (snapshot → folder → destination). Everything else is read-only. A user cannot change schedule, selection or retention from the endpoint; a technician can, from the console, with an audit entry.

Tone

Plain, specific, calm. "Backed up today at 02:10." Never "Your data is safe!" Errors say what happened and what happens next: "Waiting for power — will back up when plugged in, or at 18:00 at the latest."
